01 Approach

Build the model.
Then make it fast.

Most of my work starts with a physical system and a blank editor: a lattice of springs, a shock in a tube, a spin on a lattice, a field on an unstructured mesh.

I like the moment when the mathematics becomes something you can see — and then fast enough that you can play with it. That usually means writing the solver yourself, understanding where the cost lives, and moving it onto the GPU.

03 Research

My current research is focused on two projects: a B.Tech project on tunable bistable auxetic metasurfaces, and higher-order solution reconstruction using radial basis functions for fluid dynamics.

B.Tech project · 2025–26

Tunable bistable auxetic metasurfaces

Most standard materials, when expanded longitudinally, shrink in the perpendicular direction. Auxetic materials do the opposite: they expand in the perpendicular direction, increasing their total area or volume. They have a negative Poisson's ratio.

Auxetic metamaterials obtain this property not from the material they are made of, but from the geometry of their cells. I work on a subclass that is bi- or multi-stable, and on ways to tune the transition between states on the fly so they may be used as low-powered actuators, among many other things.

The primary investment has been the development of a fast lattice-spring solver for rapid geometry iteration — a tool for navigating the topology space toward exotic unit cells with the properties we want.

Research · Numerical methods

Higher-order reconstruction with radial basis functions

Standard techniques for reconstructing a field variable from cell-averaged quantities are often very complex — or simply not possible — on unstructured grids. A primary reason is the lack of appropriate coordinate directions. Methods that are agnostic to grid connectivity offer a viable alternative.

Approximating the field with a linear combination of radial basis functions — functions whose value depends only on distance from a centre — allows an arbitrarily high-order reconstruction, depending on the type of RBF and its support region.

The advantage over other interpolating functions is that RBFs can be used in arbitrary dimensions with minimal change. Moreover, no structure needs to be presumed for the method to work, which makes them especially attractive for unstructured grids in multi-dimensional problems.

  • Radial basis functions
  • High-order reconstruction
  • Unstructured grids

Past work · Dec 2025

Thermophotovoltaic cells for waste heat recovery

Thermophotovoltaic (TPV) cells hold significant potential for efficient heat-to-electricity conversion. Similar to solar cells, they operate by converting photons into electrical energy. The key difference lies in their spectral response: TPV cells are optimised to operate in the infrared, making them well-suited to recovering waste heat and enabling higher overall system efficiency.

This also makes TPV devices nearly fuel-agnostic, as they can be driven by heat sources such as fossil fuels, nuclear reactors or concentrated solar power. My work involved simulating various TPV cell architectures and creating models for capturing the multi-physics interactions that arise at the high temperatures these devices require.
