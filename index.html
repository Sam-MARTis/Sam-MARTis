Orbital mechanics · Systems design

Deorbiting space debris, economically

Proposed a satellite constellation of small deflector satellites equipped with lasers for ablation and large decelerator satellites to remove space debris economically in low Earth orbit. The idea is to combine cheap, distributed nudges with a smaller number of high-capability braking assets. Compared to conventional options, this basically both increases the effective area of the stopper-satellite, and reduces the requirement on the lasers from slowing down the debreis enough to deorbit it, to just nudging it very slightly into the path of the decelerator satellite.

Made the animations in Three.js to visualise the concept and the orbital mechanics involved. This is one of three animations made for the project, showing the incoming debris slowing down when hit by the decellator satellite. Instead of capturing the debris, the decelerator simply provides a Delta-V. This may not de-orbit it, but the Delta-Vs can be stacked after doing the same thing on the debris over multiple orbit passes.

B.Tech project · 2025–26

Tunable bistable auxetic metasurfaces

Most standard materials, when expanded longitudinally, shrink in the perpendicular direction. Auxetic materials do the opposite: they expand in the perpendicular direction, increasing their total area or volume. They have a negative Poisson's ratio.

Auxetic metamaterials, however, obtain this property not from the material they are made of, but from the geometry of their cells. I work on a subclass of bistable auxetic metasurfaces. I am trying to find ways to tune their response characteristics both via design parameters and on-the-fly stimuli based responses. Among many many other applications, their use as power-less actuators has great potential in aerospace and robotics.

The primary investment has been the development of a fast Lattice Spring Model (LSM) solver for rapid geometry iteration. Implementing an efficient version of the LSM has been a challenge, but it has paid off. LSMs suffer from a significant limitation that they can only model poisson's ratios close to 1/3 unless significant augmentations to the method are made. This stops them from being used in conventional elasticity problems despite their speed.

Fortunately, auxetic metasurfaces derive most of their properties from geometry, and not material properties! The underlying material's poisson's ratio is not nearly as important as the geometry and cuts made on the unit cell. So not only is the LSM fast, but it is also sufficient for the problem at hand.

The next part curretnly being worked on is to implement an algorithm to find new auxetic unit cells with desired properties. Many ways to do this, including machine learning, but gotta start with basics first and so genetic algoirthms are currently being tested.

Research · Numerical methods

Higher-order reconstruction with radial basis functions

Standard techniques for reconstructing a field variable from cell-averaged quantities are often very complex or simply not possible on an unstructured grids. A primary reason is the lack of appropriate coordinate directions. Methods that are agnostic to grid connectivity offer a viable alternative.

Approximating the field with a linear combination of radial basis functions (functions whose value depends only on distance from a centre) allows for the variation of the solution to be capture more effectively.

The advantage over other interpolating functions is that RBFs can be used in arbitrary dimensions with minimal change since they only have one 'radius' input. Moreover, no structure needs to be presumed for the method to work, as the connectivity is defined completely by the location of the function 'centers' which can be chosen independent of the grid. Thus the process is identical for structured and unstructured grids, and can be used to reconstruct a solution in a grid-agnostic manner.

They do have some problems, however. Using RBFs with global support can lead to ill-conditioned systems, and the computational cost of solving a dense system can be prohibitive. I instead use compactly supported RBFs and a local neighbourhood unique to each node. This aproach is largely geared towards hyperbolic problems which have a direction(s) of information propagation, and RBFs do not give a preference for direction. Some really cool is under works to adress this, including an edge-based neighbourhood that divides the points used for the interpolation based on whch side of the edge they are on

Past work · Dec 2025

Thermophotovoltaic cells for waste heat recovery

Thermophotovoltaic (TPV) cells hold significant potential for efficient heat-to-electricity conversion. Similar to solar cells, they operate by converting photons into electrical energy. The key difference lies in their spectral response: TPV cells are optimised to operate in the infrared, making them well-suited to recovering waste heat and enabling higher overall system efficiency.

This also makes TPV devices nearly fuel-agnostic, as they can be driven by heat sources such as fossil fuels, nuclear reactors or concentrated solar power. My work involved simulating various TPV cell architectures and creating models for capturing the multi-physics interactions that arise at the high temperatures these devices require.
